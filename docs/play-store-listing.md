the Android counterpart to [app-store-listing.md](app-store-listing.md). Field
limits are Google's and are counted in `tools/check_listing.py`.

"CrossFit" appears nowhere here either; it is a registered trademark, and
Google's metadata and impersonation policies cover it just as Apple's 5.2.1
does.

they are pasted. The paragraphs in the fenced blocks below are therefore one
line each; copy them as they are.

## Store listing

### English (United States)

**App name**

```
Cindy - Reps & Rounds
```

**Short description**

```
Hands-free rep counting for a 20-minute pull-up, push-up and squat workout
```

**Full description**

```
Cindy counts your reps so you don't have to.

Put the phone on the floor under your pull-up bar, front camera up, and start the workout: 20 minutes, as many rounds as possible of 5 pull-ups, 10 push-ups and 15 air squats. Cindy sees each rep through the front camera, counts it, beeps when an exercise is done and keeps the clock. You never touch the screen between the first rep and the last.

Everything stays on your phone
No account, no sign-up, no server – the app does not even have permission to use the internet. The camera image is analysed on the device, frame by frame, and is never stored, never recorded and never transmitted. What leaves a frame is a few numbers about your movement. Nothing else.

Built around your body, not an average
A short calibration measures how your own push-up, pull-up, squat and plank look to the camera. The thresholds that decide what counts as a rep come from that measurement, not from a table, which is why counting works whether you are tall or short and whether the phone sits near your hands or near your feet.

Your workout, your rules
Edit the round: add and remove exercises, set the reps for each, reorder them by dragging, shorten the duration, add a plank as a timed hold. One tap restores the original 5 / 10 / 15 in 20 minutes. Not sure how a movement goes? Every exercise comes with an animated demonstration.

A suggestion for next time
Every round is timed. Afterwards you get a chart of your round times, a reading of how evenly you paced yourself, and one concrete suggestion for the next session – a minute more, one more rep, a longer plank. Adopt it with one tap, or ignore it. If a harder plan sets you back, Cindy notices and steps back with you.

Know when you are ready
The start screen estimates how recovered you are, from the rest since your last session and your recent training load, and, if you allow it, from sleep, resting heart rate and heart rate variability in Health Connect. Every input is optional: without Health Connect the estimate rests on your training history alone. An optional reminder picks a moment for the next session and nudges you then, at a time of day you actually train.

Health Connect, if you want it
Off until you switch it on. Workouts are written with one segment per round, so your round structure survives in Health Connect. Workouts you finished before switching it on can be added afterwards.

What you need
An Android phone with a front camera and Android 10 or later, a pull-up bar, and a bit of floor. No watch, no chest strap, no equipment beyond the bar.

Cindy is a training aid, not a medical device. The readiness estimate is a rough guide built from training-science rules of thumb, not a diagnosis, and it is not medical advice.
```

### Data safety

**Does your app collect or share any of the required user data types? — No.**

That ends the form; the questions about encryption in transit and deletion
requests only follow a "Yes". Why "No" is correct:

- The app has no INTERNET permission (the release workflow fails the build if a
  library merges it back in), no analytics, no advertising ID and no account.
- Google counts data as *collected* only when it leaves the device. Camera
  frames, the workout history, the calibration profile and the settings stay in
  the app's own storage.
- Health Connect data is read and written on the device only.
- The CSV signal recordings leave the device only when the user shares or saves
  one through the system share sheet or file picker — user-initiated transfers
  the user expects are exempt.
- Crash reports and statistics in the Play Console come from Google Play on the
  user's device (their "Usage & diagnostics" setting), not from code in the app.

### Advertising ID

**No.** The merged release manifest has no `com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID`.

### Government apps, financial features, news apps

**No** to each.

### Health apps

Declared in two steps.

**1. Health features** — tick **Activity and fitness** only. Sleep, heart rate
and heart rate variability are inputs to the readiness estimate of a fitness
app, not a sleep- or stress-management feature of their own.

**2. Health Connect data types** — one justification per data type. Written in
English; Google reviews the declaration against the app, so every sentence
below describes something a reviewer can find in it.

**Exercise (write) — `WRITE_EXERCISE`**

```
Cindy is a workout app that counts pull-ups, push-ups and squats through the front camera. When the user turns on "Sync to Health Connect" in Settings, every finished workout is written as one ExerciseSessionRecord (calisthenics) with one segment per round, so the user's round structure is visible in Health Connect and other fitness apps. Workouts finished before sync was switched on can be exported once from Settings. Sync is off by default, and nothing is written while it is off.
```

**Active calories burned (write) — `WRITE_ACTIVE_CALORIES_BURNED`**

```
Together with the workout, Cindy writes one ActiveCaloriesBurnedRecord estimated from the workout's duration and the user's body weight (MET-based estimate), so the energy of the session is counted in Health Connect alongside the user's other activity. The record is only written when sync is on and a body weight is available; without a weight, no energy is written.
```

**Weight (read) — `READ_WEIGHT`**

```
Cindy reads the most recent weight entry to estimate the active calories of a workout it writes to Health Connect. The value is used on the device only, is not stored outside Health Connect and is never transmitted — the app has no internet permission.
```

**Sleep (read) — `READ_SLEEP`**

```
The start screen shows a readiness estimate that tells the user whether today is a good day for the workout. Last night's sleep (sleep sessions from noon of the previous day until now) is one of its inputs. The estimate is computed on the device; the data is not stored by the app and never leaves the device. Without this permission the estimate falls back to the user's workout history.
```

**Resting heart rate (read) — `READ_RESTING_HEART_RATE`**

```
Resting heart rate compared with the user's own 60-day average is one input to the readiness estimate on the start screen, which tells the user whether they have recovered enough for the next workout and when the optional reminder should suggest it. Computed on the device only; the data is not stored by the app and never transmitted. Without this permission the estimate falls back to the workout history.
```

**Heart rate variability (read) — `READ_HEART_RATE_VARIABILITY`**

```
Heart rate variability (RMSSD) compared with the user's own 60-day average is one input to the readiness estimate on the start screen, which tells the user whether they have recovered enough for the next workout. Computed on the device only; the data is not stored by the app and never transmitted. Without this permission the estimate falls back to the workout history.
```

**Read health data in the background — `READ_HEALTH_DATA_IN_BACKGROUND`**

```
Cindy offers an optional reminder that suggests the next workout once the user has recovered. A periodic background job (WorkManager, about every 12 hours) recalculates the readiness estimate from sleep, resting heart rate and heart rate variability and moves the reminder accordingly, so the notification arrives when the user is actually ready rather than at a fixed time. The permission is optional; without it the background job uses the workout history only.
```

**Read health data history — `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY`**

```
The readiness estimate compares today's resting heart rate and heart rate variability with the user's personal baseline over the last 60 days. Health Connect only returns 30 days without this permission, which would make the baseline right after installation too short to be meaningful. Only these two data types are read beyond 30 days, and only to compute the average on the device. The permission is optional.
```

## Testing

### Closed testing

A personal developer account created after 13 November 2023 must run a closed
test with at least 12 opted-in testers for 14 consecutive days before it can
apply for production access. The workflow's `alpha` track is the default closed
testing track.

**Feedback channel** (the "Feedback URL or email address" field of the track)

```
https://github.com/raddatzk/cindy/issues
```

**Message to testers** (with the opt-in link, by mail or chat)

```
Thank you for testing. What matters most in this build is whether the counting is right.

1. Run the calibration once, with the phone flat on the floor under your pull-up bar, front camera facing up.
2. Do a workout, the full 20 minutes or a shortened one from "Edit workout".
3. Afterwards, tell us how many reps you actually did in one round compared with what Cindy counted, and in which exercise it went wrong.

Also useful: which phone you have, where you put the phone, the lighting, and whether you wore a cap or glasses. If counting was off, turn on Settings › Record workouts: the next session writes a numbers-only log you can share from Settings › Recordings.
```

### Apply for production access

The Console asks about the closed test in free text. Answer from what actually
happened — how testers were recruited, what they reported, what changed as a
result, and why the app is ready. The feedback on miscounts and the fixes that
followed are the substance Google looks for.
